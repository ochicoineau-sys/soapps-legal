Privacy Policy

Foundation Match · Last updated 5 September 2026

Foundation Match runs entirely on your phone. It has no account, no server, and no permission to use the internet. Nothing you do in the app is sent anywhere, because the app has no way to send it.

What we collect

Nothing. No personal data is transmitted off your device, to us or to anyone else. There is no server to receive it, and the app holds no internet permission.

Three things are stored on your own phone, in the app's private storage, and never leave it:

StoredWhat it is
Saved shadesIdentifiers of shades from the app's built-in catalogue
Recent searchesIdentifiers of up to 8 shades you last looked at
LanguageA language code, such as en, fr or es

These identify products, not you. They contain no name, account, contact details, device identifier or location. Uninstalling the app deletes them, and the app is configured to keep them out of Android's cloud backup.

The camera

The app uses the camera in two places. Neither one takes a photograph. Both analyse the live preview in memory and discard the frames as they go. No image file is ever created.

Scanning a product label

  • Text is read from the preview entirely on your device. The recogniser's model is bundled inside the app — it downloads nothing and cannot, because the app has no internet permission.
  • What leaves the camera screen is the recognised words and any barcode digits — text, nothing more.
  • That text is used only to search the app's built-in catalogue. It is held in memory while you are on the screen, then discarded. It is never written to storage.

Estimating skin depth from a selfie

  • Six small patches of colour are sampled from fixed positions in the frame. The middle colour of each patch is taken, and the six are turned into a depth estimate from 1 to 10.
  • The picture is discarded immediately. It is never saved, never sent, and never leaves the device. What leaves the camera screen is six colour values — numbers.
  • The estimate itself is not saved either, in this or any later session.

This is not biometric processing, and the app is built so that it cannot become so. The app contains no face detection or face recognition model of any kind — not a disabled one; none is shipped. It does not locate your face, detect facial landmarks or measure facial geometry. It cannot tell one person from another, cannot recognise you, and produces no identifier or template. What it produces is a depth from 1 to 10 and a colour swatch to show you what it read.

That estimate is an estimate of colour. It is not a measurement, and it is not a dermatological, medical or health assessment of any kind.

Camera permission

Android asks for camera permission the first time you use one of these two features, not at install. Refusing costs you nothing but those two shortcuts — everything in the app can be reached by typing instead. You can revoke it at any time in Android settings. The app declares no storage permission, because it stores no images.

What the app does not do

  • No account, no sign-in, no profile.
  • No analytics, telemetry, crash reporting or usage tracking of our own. The app has an analytics interface and the version that ships does nothing at all when called: nothing is recorded and nothing is sent. See the note on third-party components below.
  • No advertising, no ad SDK, no advertising identifier.
  • No location, contacts, microphone, files or photo library access.
  • No cookies and no web tracking. The interface is rendered locally from the app package, not loaded from a website.
  • No sale or sharing of data — there is no data leaving the device to sell or share.
  • No internet permission at all. This is enforced by Android, not by our good intentions.

Third-party components

The app includes third-party software libraries for the camera, on-device text recognition and barcode reading. None of them sends anything anywhere in this app, because none of them can: there is no internet permission. The text recogniser's model ships inside the app and runs offline.

Being complete about that: the text recogniser also brings Google's own telemetry and scheduling components with it. The two network permissions they ask for are explicitly removed from this app's manifest, and a build check fails if either comes back. The scheduling components are still packaged, because the recogniser depends on them — not because this app uses them. They cannot send anything, because there is no internet permission, and that is enforced by Android rather than by configuration. Whether they write anything into the app's own private storage has not been checked on a device; if they did, it could still never leave your phone, and clearing the app's storage or uninstalling would remove it.

Shopping links

The app does not currently link to any retailer, and shows “no retailer for your region yet” instead. If retailer links are added later, tapping one would open your own browser and leave the app; what happens after that would be governed by that retailer's privacy policy. This policy will be updated before any such link ships.

Children

The app is not directed at children. It collects nothing, so it collects nothing from children either.

Your rights

Because nothing leaves your device, there is no copy of your data for anyone to disclose, export, correct or erase on request. The data described above is on your phone, under your control:

  • See it or change it — saved shades can be removed one at a time in the app, and recent searches have a “Clear” control.
  • Erase all of it — clear the app's storage in Android settings, or uninstall the app.

Rights under the UK GDPR and EU GDPR — access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection — apply to personal data held by a controller. Olivier Chicoineau holds none. You are welcome to write to o.chicoineau@gmail.com regardless, and we will answer.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of the app changes what it does — adds a network feature, analytics, or retailer links — this policy will be updated before that version is published, and the change described here.
